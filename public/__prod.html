Where enforcement lives

In the contract that holds the money. The server can refuse to sign, but it can't make the contract accept a trade outside the owner's limits.

  1. 1Agent

    Proposes a trade

    Pair and size only. Anything it says about its own fees, stop-loss or confidence is ignored. It can be an LLM, a script, or a hijacked version of either.

  2. 2Guard server

    Prices it and signs it

    It quotes the trade on the DEX pool itself, measures fees and price impact, sets a minimum output 0.5% under the quote, values it with the live NEAR price, and signs it for this vault only, once, for two minutes. It has no trading strategy.

  3. 3GuardVault contract

    Checks its own limits

    Caller, signature, freshness, replay, token and venue allowlists, per-trade cap in USD and in raw tokens, daily cap, fee ceiling, the owner's price floor, kill switch. Above $4 it waits for the owner.

  4. 4Venue

    Fills or refunds

    The contract writes the Ref Finance swap itself. Ref pays out at least the minimum to the vault, or reverts and the tokens come back. No trade has a recipient field.

Worst case if our guard server is hacked: it can sign trades right at the owner's price floor, 7.6% under today's pool price, up to $4 a trade without the owner. That's about $0.31 a trade and $7.65 a day on a $100 daily cap, until the owner or guardian pauses. Tighter floors or a lower cap shrink it.

Who can move these funds

  • No full-access keys. Nobody can sign a transfer out of this account.
  • Agent key ed25519:GxC8Bf…F3hfcG: may only call execute() on the vault, with 2.988 NEAR left for gas.
  • Owner intentrail-owner-39ae84.testnet approves big trades and can tighten limits at once. Withdrawals and looser limits wait 10 minutes. Anyone holding this key could queue a withdrawal to itself.
  • Guardian intentrail-guardian-39ae84.testnet can pause and veto anything queued. It can't move funds.

Why this needs NEAR

Keys that can only do one thing

A NEAR function-call access key is limited by the protocol to one contract, chosen methods and a gas budget. The vault installs the agent's key itself: execute() only. On most chains you'd need a smart-contract wallet and a module system to get the same guarantee.

Accounts nobody can sign for

After setup the vault's only full-access key was deleted. Its funds move only when its code says so, and anyone can confirm that from the chain.

Contracts that call DEXes directly

The vault writes its own Ref Finance swap and gets the result back in a callback. If Ref refuses or refunds, the vault's daily spend is restored.

Mainnet: the contract signs intents through chain signatures

On testnet the vault trades on Ref Finance and the guard prices every trade on that same pool. For NEAR Intents on mainnet, funds held in intents.near move only with a signed intent. A key that a server or a person holds would bypass every check above. So the vault won't hold one. The contract checks the intent, then asks NEAR's chain-signatures MPC network to sign it. No hot key exists anywhere.

A preflight check for any agent

Other agents can use the guard as a preflight check. Send the pair and size. The guard prices the trade from a live NEAR Intents 1Click quote and returns every check with its numbers.

This API prices on NEAR Intents 1Click because that's where other agents' trades settle on mainnet. The vault itself prices every trade on the pool it actually trades on.

It ignores anything an agent says about itself, such as its fees, stop-loss or votes, and lists those fields under ignoredFields. Limits that need memory, like the daily cap, live in the vault contract.
